Privacy

Last updated October 19, 2026

The short version: Dot Awkward doesn't collect anything about you. There are no accounts and no analytics, and your games, scores and settings stay on your phone. The one exception is the Small Plates Android app from Google Play, which shows a small banner ad served by Google AdMob; Google receives some information to show it, explained below. The websites have no ads.

What this covers

This policy covers the games made by Dot Awkward, Small Plates and Potting Bench, in their web versions and their Android apps, plus the Dot Awkward website.

What we collect

Dot Awkward itself collects nothing. The games never send information to Dot Awkward, don't ask for an account, an email address or your location, and don't include analytics. The web versions, and Potting Bench, contain no advertising code. The Small Plates Android app (version 1.2 and later) includes Google's advertising code for one banner ad; see "Ads in the Small Plates app" below.

Ads in the Small Plates app

The Small Plates app from Google Play shows one banner ad along the bottom of the screen. It sits in its own space and never covers the game. There are no ads that pop up between rounds, no ads that make you wait, and nothing you have to watch to unlock anything.

The banner comes from Google AdMob. To choose, show and count ads, and to prevent fraud, Google's ad code on your phone sends Google:

  • Your phone's advertising ID, a resettable ID Android gives each phone for ads.
  • Your IP address and rough location worked out from it (country or city, not your exact location).
  • Device and app details, such as the phone model, Android version, language and the fact that the ad is shown in Small Plates.
  • How you interact with the ad, such as whether it was shown or tapped, and crash information from the ad code.

Google may use this to show personalized ads, depending on your choices. Dot Awkward doesn't receive any of it, only totals such as how many ads were shown. Nothing from your games, names or stats is shared. See how Google uses information from apps that use its services and Google's privacy policy.

Your choices. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, the app asks for your consent the first time you open it, and you can change your answer any time under Menu → Settings → Ad privacy choices. Anywhere, you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalized ads, in your phone's settings (usually Settings → Google → Ads, or Settings → Privacy → Ads). The ads still show if you opt out, but they're based on less information.

What stays on your phone

To remember your progress, the games save a few things in your phone's own storage:

  • Your games in progress, scores, stats and streaks.
  • Any player names you type in, such as the two names in a game of Small Plates for two.
  • Your settings, like sound, vibration and light or dark mode.
  • In Potting Bench, your garden and the plants you're caring for.

This information never leaves your phone unless you move it yourself. Deleting the app, or clearing the website's data in your browser, erases it.

Moving and sharing

  • Transfer codes and backups. Small Plates' "Move your games" code and Potting Bench's backup file contain your saved progress. They only go where you copy, paste or save them.
  • Sharing a result. When you share a Daily Special result, your phone's share menu sends exactly the text you see to the app and person you choose. Dot Awkward doesn't see it.

Services we rely on

  • GitHub Pages hosts the websites. Like any web host, it may log basic technical information, such as your IP address, to keep the service running and secure. See GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Google Fonts supplies the lettering on the Dot Awkward website, so your browser contacts Google when you visit it. The games themselves carry their own fonts. See Google's privacy policy.
  • Google AdMob serves the banner ad in the Small Plates Android app, as described above.
  • Google Play handles downloading the Android apps and any purchases, under Google's privacy policy. Dot Awkward doesn't receive your payment details.

Children

The games are made for a general audience and aren't directed at children under 13. Dot Awkward doesn't knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. The ads in the Small Plates app are limited to family-friendly content (Google's PG rating or milder).

If this changes

If another app adds ads or in-app purchases, we'll update this page before that version comes out and explain exactly what any ad or payment service receives. Dot Awkward games will never show ads that pop up between rounds, make you wait, cover the game, or have to be watched to unlock something. The web versions stay ad-free.
